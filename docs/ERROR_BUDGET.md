# Error budget — where the complete system fails

This page answers one question across the whole pipeline: **when a fine is
wrong, which stage was responsible, and how much does each stage matter?** It is
a consolidation of measurements that live in other pages, gathered here so the
failure surface can be read in one place. Every number is generated by a
committed script over a committed result file; nothing here is estimated by
hand.

Two different kinds of evidence appear below and they must not be conflated:

- **Model metrics** — the detector measured on a held-out image split
  (`traffic_model-2` on `traffic-4class` test, de-leaked). Real detections,
  real ground truth, small same-pool set.
- **System sensitivity** — a *model-free* fault-injection study
  (`evaluate_pipeline.py`, [modules/pipeline_eval.py](../modules/pipeline_eval.py)):
  each stage is degraded by an equal 30% over 20 seeded trials and the
  end-to-end F1 drop is measured. This ranks how much the system **depends on**
  each stage. **It is not** a claim about how often a stage fails in the field —
  that needs a labelled video corpus nobody in this project has.

Sources: [MODEL_EVALUATION.md](MODEL_EVALUATION.md),
[END_TO_END_EVALUATION.md](END_TO_END_EVALUATION.md),
[ERROR_ANALYSIS.md](ERROR_ANALYSIS.md), and the generated
[eval/results/pipeline_evaluation.json](../eval/results/pipeline_evaluation.json)
and [eval/results/tracking_comparison.json](../eval/results/tracking_comparison.json).

## The one-line budget

At equal fault rates, end-to-end fine F1 depends on the stages in this order
(`eval/results/pipeline_evaluation.json`, baseline F1 = 1.000, seed 7, 20
trials):

| stage injected | what breaks | mean F1 | F1 drop | share of measured sensitivity |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **OCR** | plate characters corrupted | 0.547 | **−0.453** | **76.8%** |
| detector class | helmet / no-helmet flipped | 0.910 | −0.090 | 15.3% |
| detector recall | boxes vanish | 0.957 | −0.043 | 7.3% |
| plate recall | plate missed on a frame | 0.997 | −0.003 | 0.6% |

**OCR corruption dominates by 5× over the next stage.** Everything below is the
per-stage detail behind that ranking, in pipeline order.

## 1. Detector — the ceiling

The detector bounds everything downstream: a violation never emitted is a
violation the rest of the pipeline cannot recover. Measured on the de-leaked
test split ([MODEL_EVALUATION.md](MODEL_EVALUATION.md) §2–4):

- **mAP@50 0.727, mAP@50-95 0.532** overall.
- **`WithHelmet` is the worst class: mAP@50 0.387, F1 0.519**, on only **27**
  test instances (high variance). The confusion is genuine — **8**
  `WithoutHelmet`→`WithHelmet` and **3** the other way in the error-analysis
  pass — and it is the one class where a wrong call is a wrong *fine*.
- Confidence is discriminative for `WithoutHelmet` (correct-vs-wrong separation
  strong) but carries **no signal for `TripleRiding`** — a 0.9 detection there
  is no likelier correct than a 0.5 one, so that class is never thresholded.

**Why the detector is the ceiling and stays it:** the fault-injection budget
above shows detector faults (class + recall) are together only ~23% of measured
sensitivity, *far* behind OCR — but that is sensitivity at equal fault rates,
not failure frequency. In absolute terms the detector's own accuracy (mAP@50
0.727) is the hard limit on what can be detected at all. A strong pipeline
around a mediocre detector is still bounded by the detector. This is exactly why
every flag is a **candidate for human review, never a verdict**.

Root-cause analysis of the `WithHelmet` weakness and the evidence-based data fix
(dedup + leakage removal, [build_train_split.py](../build_train_split.py)) are
in [ERROR_ANALYSIS.md](ERROR_ANALYSIS.md) §1 and the retraining decision in
[MODEL_VERSIONING.md](MODEL_VERSIONING.md).

## 2. Tracking — measured clean after the association fix

Tracking is **not** currently a source of end-to-end error. Over 8 targeted
crossing scenarios (`evaluate_tracking.py`, two crossing at two speeds, three
crossing, partial overlap at two gaps, long overlap, occlusion recovery, late
plate during a crossing), with the shipped config:

| | old config | shipped config |
|---|---:|---:|
| ID switches | 13 | **0** |
| fragmentations | 4 | **1** |
| spurious merged frames | 22 | **1** |

The one remaining fragmentation is the deliberate full-occlusion recovery case,
where the vehicle is genuinely absent for 3 frames and the same identity is
recovered afterwards. The constant-velocity motion model in
[modules/vehicle_tracker.py](../modules/vehicle_tracker.py) was **not** replaced
with a Kalman filter, because the diagnosis (below) showed the tracker was never
the bottleneck.

## 3. Association — was the crossing weakness; now measured clean

The documented `crossing` weakness was an **association** fault, not a tracking
fault. At the crossing frames two vehicles' body boxes reach IoU 0.43; the old
`body_merge_iou` of 0.3 unioned two different vehicles into one body **before
the tracker ran**, so every ID switch landed on a frame where the tracker was
handed one anchor for two vehicles. Raising the merge thresholds to the
canonical 0.5 IoU / 0.8 containment removes it (`eval/results/tracking_comparison.json`):

| | old config | shipped config |
|---|---:|---:|
| association accuracy | 0.760 (136/179) | **0.989 (177/179)** |
| wrong associations | 20 | **1** |
| fines TP / FP / FN | 10 / 5 / 2 | **12 / 0 / 0** |

The last row is the one that matters: the old thresholds issued **5
false-positive fines and missed 2 real violations** on overlapping traffic. The
fix is pinned by a regression suite ([tests/test_tracking_eval.py](../tests/test_tracking_eval.py))
so the three legitimate merges (sub-part inside a rider, helmet contradiction,
triple body) cannot be broken silently. Full reasoning: the commit message on
`fix(association)` and [END_TO_END_EVALUATION.md](END_TO_END_EVALUATION.md) §8.

## 4. OCR — the dominant dependency, and NOT MEASURED in the field

OCR is **76.8% of measured sensitivity** — a corrupted plate misattributes a
fine to a real but *wrong* vehicle, and the system has no way to notice. A
*missing* plate, by contrast, is nearly free (0.6%): other frames recover it.
The stabilizer's willingness to abstain is a defence against the expensive
failure and a cost paid in the cheap one.

**Field OCR accuracy is NOT MEASURED.** There is no labelled OCR dataset in this
project, so no field character/exact-match accuracy is asserted anywhere. What
*is* verified:

- The scoring logic (edit distance, normalized/char accuracy, structure
  validity, temporal voting) is unit-tested model-free
  ([tests/test_ocr_eval.py](../tests/test_ocr_eval.py),
  [tests/test_plate_recognizer.py](../tests/test_plate_recognizer.py)).
- The end-to-end evaluator path (CSV → image → EasyOCR → metrics) now runs on
  real image files, exercised against a **synthetic** sanity set
  ([make_ocr_sanity_set.py](../make_ocr_sanity_set.py)); its metrics degrade the
  right way as images worsen. Those numbers are **not** a benchmark — vector
  glyphs on a flat background — and are stamped synthetic so they can never be
  read as field accuracy.

To measure field accuracy, supply a labelled set in the documented schema
`image_path,plate,condition` (conditions: clean/blur/angle/lowlight/small/
occluded) and run `evaluate_ocr.py --labels <csv>`. See §"OCR benchmark schema"
below.

## 5. Temporal confirmation — what it buys, measured

Temporal confirmation is the mechanism that keeps a single wrong frame from
becoming a fine. Against a naive single-frame fining policy on identical
detections (`eval/results/pipeline_evaluation.json`):

| detection noise | naive F1 (mean FP) | pipeline F1 (mean FP) | gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.00 | 0.912 (1.50) | 1.000 (0.00) | +0.088 |
| 0.20 | 0.820 (4.08) | 0.962 (0.23) | +0.142 |
| 0.40 | 0.808 (4.47) | 0.851 (0.72) | +0.043 |

For the OCR plate decision specifically, temporal voting trades coverage for
correctness — **accuracy-when-answered 0.98 at 0.51 coverage**, versus
best-single-frame 0.70 at full coverage. Abstaining on half the sequences to be
right 98% of the time when it does answer is the correct trade for a system that
issues fines: a wrong plate tickets an innocent rider; an abstention only misses
one.

**Its limit:** with exactly two conflicting but structurally valid readings the
agreement score is 0.5, which clears the confidence floor, so the stabilizer
elects rather than abstains. Three-way disagreement correctly abstains. This is
the weakest point of the voting rule, pinned by a test so it cannot drift.

## 6. Speed — an estimate, and perspective-blind without calibration

Speed is measured on synthetic constant-speed trajectories with known ground
truth through the real estimator (`eval/results/pipeline_evaluation.json`,
[END_TO_END_EVALUATION.md](END_TO_END_EVALUATION.md) §7):

| motion | best calibration | MAE (km/h) | overspeed recall |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| toward camera | homography | **11.3** | 1.00 |
| toward camera | constant ppm | ~42 | **0.00** |
| lateral | linear plane | **0.87** | 1.00 |

For motion toward the camera — the common enforcement geometry — a single
pixels-per-metre value is perspective-blind and **misses every overspeeder**.
The homography is the only usable option, and even at its best its MAE is
**11.3 km/h**. That is why speed is labelled an **estimate** everywhere, never
radar-grade, and an uncalibrated camera reports no speed at all rather than a
meaningless number.

## OCR benchmark schema (for a real measurement)

A future labelled OCR benchmark drops in with no code change:

```
image_path,plate,condition
images/frame_00012.jpg,MH12AB1234,clean
images/frame_00048.jpg,KA05MN6789,blur
```

- `image_path` — resolved relative to the CSV's directory.
- `plate` — ground-truth string.
- `condition` — one of `clean|blur|angle|lowlight|small|occluded`; drives the
  per-condition breakdown.

Run `python3 evaluate_ocr.py --labels <csv>` (or `--compare-preprocess` to rank
preprocessing pipelines). Until such a set exists, **field OCR accuracy remains
NOT MEASURED**.

## What this budget is not

- It is **not** a field failure-frequency table. It ranks *dependence* at equal
  fault rates. A stage with high sensitivity is where a real failure would hurt
  most, not necessarily where failures actually occur.
- The detector metrics are on a small, same-pool held-out split. They do not
  claim real-world accuracy; see the leakage/imbalance caveats in
  [DATASET.md](DATASET.md).
- Perfect scores on the synthetic system scenarios are a **ceiling, not a
  measurement** — the suite was written by the same author as the pipeline and
  contains no motion blur, crowds, or confidence noise.
